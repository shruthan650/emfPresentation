How should an organization be structured?

Organizational structure is the framework that assigns responsibilities, reporting relationships, communication pathways, and decision authority. A good fit connects the way work is organized to the organization’s goals, people, and operating environment.

  • Authority
  • Communication
  • Decision-making
  • Coordination

Why compare structures?

Organizations have traditionally used formal hierarchies to coordinate large workforces. Global markets, knowledge work, specialist partners, and digital communication have broadened the available choices. This presentation compares four common models and the trade-offs each one creates.

Tall

Several management levels, a clear chain of command, and close supervision.

Flat

Few management tiers, broad spans of control, and more direct communication.

Network

A focused core coordinates specialist suppliers and external partners.

Virtual

Distributed people coordinate through digital platforms and shared workflows.
